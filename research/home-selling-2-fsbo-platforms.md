# FSBO Listing Platforms — Cost, Syndication, and Agent Exposure

Research date: 2026-09-04. Verified against vendor pricing/help pages where possible. Claims sourced only from 2024-or-earlier pages are flagged (possibly stale).

## Naming note: "HomeList"

There is **no active FSBO platform at homelist.com or homelist.co** as of 2026-09-04:

- `homelist.com` is an unrelated Shopify store selling eye-protection LED lamps ([verified directly](https://homelist.com/)).
- `homelist.co` is **for sale on GoDaddy's aftermarket** — the domain's own llms.txt confirms it is parked and listed at [forsale.godaddy.com/forsale/homelist.co](https://homelist.co/llms.txt).

The platform the user almost certainly means is **HomeLister** (homelister.com), the well-known flat-fee FSBO/MLS service. HomeLister is documented below; the dead "HomeList" domains are noted for disambiguation in case the user saw it in a comparison list.

## Syndication mechanics — what actually reaches what

- **Free portal listings (ZFSBO, FSBO.com) never touch the MLS.** They sit on the portal's own inventory. Zillow FSBO → Zillow + Trulia only. FSBO.com → FSBO.com + (per Redfin's documented pipeline) Redfin mirror after ~48h.
- **MLS is the only route to Realtor.com, IDX, and buyers' agents.** A licensed broker must file the listing; flat-fee services are thin broker wrappers doing exactly that.
- **Google Maps is not a real estate listings surface for individuals.** Google's Home Listings Ads (via ComeHome/HouseCanary feeds) are agent-run Local Services Ads in Google Search, explicitly **not** Google Maps, and require a verified Google Business Profile + LSA campaign tied to an agent — FSBO owners can't run them ([The Close](https://theclose.com/news-google-home-listing-ads/), [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026-06-29/google-home-listing-ads-expanding-how-real-estate-agents-participate/)). A home doesn't "appear on Google Maps" via any FSBO workflow; only the address's existing place data does.
- **Consequence of dual-listing:** if you list on ZFSBO and later add a flat-fee MLS filing, Zillow may replace the FSBO record with the MLS one (HomeLister's help center instructs removing ZFSBO first).

## What MLS participation rules mean for an FSBO seller

Verified against primary NAR sources:

1. **Owners cannot log into the MLS directly.** MLS participation is a privilege of licensed broker members. "Flat-fee MLS" means a licensed broker files your listing for a flat fee — you are a client of that broker, not an MLS participant. (Consistent with NAR's policy framing, below, and every vendor's documentation.)
2. **NAR Clear Cooperation Policy (Policy Statement 8.00, Participants' Rights §17, effective 2026-01-01):** the listing broker must submit a property to the MLS **within one business day** of any public marketing ([primary](https://www.nar.realtor/handbook-on-multiple-listing-policy/participants-rights-section-17-clear-cooperation-policy-statement-8-00); policy page: [nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/mls-clear-cooperation-policy](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/mls-clear-cooperation-policy)). "Public marketing" explicitly includes public-facing websites, yard signs, digital marketing, and email blasts.
   - **Practical reading for an FSBO seller who self-markets first** (posts to Zillow, FSBO.com, yard sign, Craigslist) and only later engages a flat-fee broker: the moment you publicly market, a 1-business-day clock runs on any broker you subsequently retain. NAR's own office-exclusive/pre-marketing guidance says owner-listed properties discussed with outside brokers should use one-to-one broker-to-broker communication with explicit seller authorization ([primary](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/mls-policy/office-exclusive-listings/pre-marketing-guidance)). In practice, flat-fee services will file promptly; the risk is a broker disputing whether your DIY marketing triggered the rule.
3. **Zillow/Trulia enforce their own Listing Access Standards (introduced April 2025, updated 2026-03-17):** publicly marketed listings must be broadly accessible; gated/broker-exclusive listings are blocked for the life of the listing agreement. Crucially, **FSBO listings are explicitly exempt** — "For sale by owner listings are still able to be entered directly onto our sites" ([primary](https://www.zillow.com/pro/agents-know-listing-access-standards/)). So a pure ZFSBO listing is legitimate on Zillow with no MLS involvement.
4. **Commission is decoupled from MLS rules.** Post-2024 (NAR settlement), the MLS/IDX does not obligate buyer-side compensation; what a buyer's agent is paid is a private contract between buyer and agent, independent of whether your home is on the MLS. MLS rules govern *cooperation* (access to the listing), not *compensation*. [SECONDARY for settlement details — widely documented; primary settlement terms: NAR/NAF settlement, 2024]
5. **Local MLS rules vary** — photo limits, minimum listing duration, owner-occupied vs. investor rules, and whether "FSBO" is a distinct MLS status are all set by each regional MLS. Any flat-fee service is bound by your specific MLS's rules; the national NAR policy is a floor, not a substitute for the local rules. Verify your local MLS's FSBO/flat-fee policy before paying.

## Developer/self-hosting lens — summary

- **No platform in this space offers a public API, webhooks, or data export for sellers.** All self-service is web-dashboard-only; contact details are gated by design (Zillow hides inquirer info; portals deliberately do not expose buyer-inquiry data via any API — consistent with S4's findings).
- **The only "API" that matters is the MLS feed itself**, and access is broker-gated.
- **Privacy:** free portal listings (ZFSBO, FSBO.com) are the most data-lightweight options — your data lives on their portal, no MLS record created. Flat-fee MLS services create a durable MLS record plus multiple portal copies you cannot individually retract.
- **Cheapest credible stack for a homelab-minded seller** (cost, verified 2026): ZFSBO free (unlimited photos, video, Zillow+Trulia) + a $95–$99 true-flat MLS filing (HomeRise/Beycome class) if buyer-agent reach is wanted. Total: $95–$99. Hybrid tiers (Houzeo etc.) cost 3–10× more once closing percentages are counted.
